Methodology

Qualitative recruiting-fit assessment for Sara, not a statistical probability model. It combines (1) her profile — 1.71 m setter, Romanian U17 national champion, Best Setter U17, U19 experience and strong academics; (2) evidence from current rosters, especially setter heights and whether the program has recently recruited setters in her height range; (3) recent team strength and conference performance, because stronger teams are generally harder to break into; and (4) practical program context. ‘Strong athletic fit’ means her profile looks clearly plausible for that volleyball program; ‘Good athletic fit’ means the fit is credible but more competitive; ‘Athletic reach’ means she should still email the school, but the volleyball level or roster context makes it materially tougher.

What the chart encodings mean

  • Color = athletic fit: blue Strong, orange Good, green Reach.
  • Shape = division: circles are NCAA D1 and triangles NCAA D3.
  • Admissions reach is kept separate from volleyball fit. A school can be a Strong athletic fit and still an Extreme admissions reach.
  • Academic rank is the 2026 U.S. News position in the institution’s applicable category. The school universe was curated using the 2024–26 academic screen discussed in the research; always re-check rankings before final application decisions.

How the volleyball-strength score is constructed

Volleyball strength (0–100) is an internal comparison scale created for this recruiting project. Its purpose is to give the vertical axis a practical measure of how difficult the volleyball environment is likely to be, so that an academically attractive school with a relatively accessible team can be distinguished from an academically similar school with a nationally or conference-dominant program. It is not an NCAA RPI, AVCA ranking, Massey rating, power rating, predicted win probability or official measure used by coaches.

1. Recent conference performance

The starting point is the most recent conference record or conference standing available in the research, usually the 2025 season. Conference winning percentage is the strongest numerical anchor: a team winning roughly 80–90% of its conference matches will normally sit far above a team winning 10–30%.

2. Program / conference context

A raw conference record is not perfectly comparable across leagues or divisions. The assessment therefore also considers whether the program competes in a particularly strong volleyball environment, is a recurring conference contender, or operates at a level where roster competition is materially tougher.

3. Recent competitive level

Where known, conference-tournament performance, titles, top finishes and the general recent competitiveness of the program are used as context. The score is meant to represent the current recruiting environment, not the historical prestige of the school’s volleyball program.

4. Deliberately approximate

The score is a recruiting-comparison proxy, not a precise formula with fixed percentage weights. A difference such as 72 vs. 68 should not be interpreted as scientifically meaningful. The broad ordering and bands matter more than a few points of separation.

Practical interpretation:

  • 80–100: very strong recent program / conference leader territory; generally a substantially harder volleyball roster to break into.
  • 60–79: strong or upper-half program; credible recruiting target only if the athlete matches the position-specific standard and roster need.
  • 40–59: competitive middle range; often a more plausible athletic target, depending on setter profile and recruiting class.
  • 20–39: lower recent conference performance; national-level credentials can become more differentiated here.
  • 0–19: very weak recent conference results in the season used; this does not mean the team or individual players are weak, only that the recent competitive results used for this project were at the low end of this target universe.
Important limitation: the 0–100 score should be used to compare broad volleyball difficulty, especially alongside the setter-height evidence. It should not be used as a direct D1-vs-D3 ranking, and it does not account perfectly for injuries, roster turnover, strength of schedule, incoming recruits or a coach’s specific need for a setter in Sara’s graduation year. Those factors can make a nominally stronger program a better recruiting opportunity than a lower-scoring one.
